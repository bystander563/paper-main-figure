# Scientific schematic grammar

Choose the representation from the approved mechanism and audience. Record the
takeaway, main objects, important relations and source-backed innovation before
choosing a panel layout. Examples calibrate visual quality; they do not prescribe
a universal number of panels, colors, icons or formulas.

## Show objects changing

Favor a small family of recognizable scientific objects:
- sequences and states with aligned positions or highlighted differences;
- model blocks whose relationships show actual computation;
- features, codebooks, masks or graphs when the method really contains them;
- selected members, target signals and outputs placed on the objects they affect.

Repeat object identity across stages so readers see what changes. Vary marks
only to show a real distinction. Treat schematic snippets, partial states and
probability marks as illustrations; explain them and never imply measured values.

An input must preserve its granularity. If the model receives a sentence,
highlight or extract that sentence from any contextual document before the
model-input arrow. Show train-only information separately from deployment.

## Use hierarchy without a dashboard

Large lightly tinted regions establish grouping; internal scientific objects
carry detail. Short labels name them. Use module borders only where ownership,
state or grouping matters. Do not frame every word or mathematical identifier.
A neural network icon is not evidence of the architecture; use the actual
block structure or a clearly conventional encoder abstraction.

Keep comparative conditions and exact implementation counts in a modest
footer or caption unless they are the central mechanism. Explicit exclusions
can be summarized by a clear deployment boundary plus the caption.

## Notation and typography

Use field-standard labels and source-defined notation. Distinguish a displayed
equation from an identifier such as a state index or probability name. Both
need meanings and source anchors; only displayed equations consume the formula
budget. Default an overview to zero equations and justify any one retained.
Long objectives belong in the method. A formula-centered paper may use
FORMULA_CORE while preserving input/output relations and readability.

Typography roles, not arbitrary local choices, determine font size and weight.
Measure actual fonts. Repeat a role consistently; use a suitable math font for
math rather than substituting random text fonts.

## Calibration examples

The following are visual observations, not rules or borrowed science:
- [OneSearch Figure 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03236v1): heterogeneous micro-
  diagrams inside a stable macro board. Preprint status must not be relabeled
  as conference acceptance without evidence.
- [OneSearch-V2 Figure 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24422v2): repeated tokens
  and selections expose the process. One equation is subordinate to the diagram.
- [DINOv2 Meets Text, CVPR 2025, Figure 2](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Jose_DINOv2_Meets_Text_A_Unified_Framework_for_Image-_and_Pixel-Level_CVPR_2025_paper.html):
  unequal panel widths follow pretraining, alignment and inference content.
- [HippoRAG 2, ICML 2025, Figure 2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/gutierrez25a.html):
  offline/online lanes preserve node identity and have a clear merge/split path.

Inspect at thumbnail and placed size, then compare information retention and
scan order at equal physical width. Do not demand extra invented machinery to
look as complex as these examples.
